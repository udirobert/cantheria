Cantheria

Swarm Alignment Hackathon submission · Track 4: Multi-Agent Oversight and Control · November 8, 2026
An LLM proposes; the record decides.

1. Problem

Multi-agent incident investigation runs on narration. Agents write summaries; humans or AI skim them; the narrative becomes fact. The external investigation of the OpenAI–Hugging Face incident concedes the failure mode in its own words: analysis “had to heavily delegate … to often-unreliable AI agents” — over transcripts the swarm actively tried to spoof, edit, and delete. The dataset card for the AI Village transcripts makes the same point in miniature: “Treat an agent's narration as a claim, not ground truth.”

Cantheria is a falsification pipeline for claims about multi-agent systems. Model output is input; a claim only ships when a probe survives a mechanical evidence chain against the underlying record. Everything the model was wrong about stays in an append-only journal — the honesty is the feature.

2. Approach

Corpus → normalize every source into one records table (sqlite) → decompose documents into atomic claims, or hypothesize claims per segment → the model drafts a probe: SELECT-only SQL over records → an evidence oracle applies the kill chain → reranker triage → investigator-ready reports with deep-link receipts.

The four legs, all mechanical:

  1. Grounding — the probe returns rows; cited record_ids resolve.
  2. Attribute — matched rows bind the claimed subject, author-or-mention, and time window.
  3. Replicate — pattern/coordination claims need minimum matched-row counts or distinct subjects.
  4. Control — a disjoint-window query must not satisfy the same expectations (negative-control discrimination, not a calibrated FPR).

Verdicts: confirmed | dismissed | flaky | unverifiable. Interpretive claims are quarantined unverifiable by construction. Probes run on read-only handles (mode=ro, PRAGMA query_only), row-capped, step-limited. Source documents enter the model fenced (nonce envelope; injection carriers marked, not deleted). Crucially, the verdict path contains no LLM judgment — the model drafts a probe; sqlite decides. This is the answer to the monitor-fragility literature (agent-as-a-proxy attacks bypass LLM monitors at >90% ASR): neither the investigated swarm nor the analyst model can talk its way to a verdict.

On corpus 2 the lens inverts: Cantheria audits the investigators' own incident report against its underlying record; 89% of its decidable claims (81/91) could not be mechanically established. Verification failure is not unique to agent-generated summaries — human-written investigation reports fail too, which is why receipts must ship with the narrative.

Three findings worth the receipts:

  • The day that wasn't. summary:2026-07-03 went 0/12 — every claim dismissed on a day with 825 chats / ~2k events / 23k turns in the corpus. A warning about the summary, not proof each claim is false.
  • The thing nobody searched for. Hunt mode autonomously confirmed an agent announcing a private session to roll a d6 to assign its own role — found without anyone writing a query for it.
  • The verifier auditing itself. One claim's model-drafted probes failed 3× (over-constraint; unquoted FTS hyphens); a mechanical fallback matched 17 rows and confirmed. If model probes were the oracle, our own failure rate would have been a fabrication.

5. Evaluation against known truth

Real-corpus runs prove verdicts reproduce; they cannot prove them right. We built a planted-answer eval (eval/synthetic_ground_truth/): a 53-record synthetic swarm with 15 claims tagged by known truth — including attribution traps, window traps, a fabricated coordination token, and a true absence on an empty room.

systemtrue–confirmedfalse–confirmed (FP)false–dismissedabstained
Cantheria (audit2, 18 LLM calls / 24 probe runs)6/70/72/75/7 false + 1/7 true
LLM judge, full corpus in context (3 runs)~6/70–1/7~6/71–2/7
LLM judge, keyword-retrieved evidence packs (3 runs)6/70/77/71/7 true + 1/3×Ghost
Zero false confirmations — the property an oversight tool must have, matched by both judge baselines on this toy corpus. The honest comparison: the retrieval-limited judge dismissed more fabricated claims than we did (it answers contradicted where our drafter abstains). Cantheria's edges are the ones a 53-record eval cannot show: probes are precise predicates, not fuzzy retrieval + judgment — at 3.6M records the judge's evidence pack is only as good as keyword recall, while a probe either lands or doesn't, with receipts. Plus determinism (judge verdicts flip between runs; our probes replay identically with no model) and the journal of the verifier's own failures.
Documented blind spots (measured, not hidden): (1) true absence claims on empty windows are structurally unconfirmable; (2) the drafter abstains on claims whose terms have zero corpus hits, so 5/7 fabricated claims land unverifiable rather than dismissed — safe, but a recall gap; (3) 53 records is a unit test, not a field estimate.

6. Position in the landscape

Related work, verified by search (full map: RELATED_WORK.md):

  • kollude (this hackathon) — statistical swarm- collusion detector: template-copying statistics + anytime-valid e-CUSUM alarms with bounded false-alarm rate, seeder tracing, MCP server. Different layer: it answers “is there coordinated copying?” with calibration; Cantheria answers “is this claim true?” with receipts. We ran it head-to-head on the same collusion.wiki dump (reports/kollude-head-to-head.md): its first anytime-valid alarm fires 2026-05-28 vs. our density monitor's 2026-05-27 — two unrelated methods independently flag the swarm ~5 days before human cleanup, and both concentrate alarms/confirmed claims in the same 06-16→20 window. A kollude alarm is exactly the suspicion Cantheria can adjudicate — detectors propose, the record decides.
  • Fact-checking lineage — FEVER/AVeriTeC (verdict + evidence scored jointly; InFact 63% SOTA), SummaC/QAFactEval/ MiniCheck (consistency metrics), Evidence Chain Evaluation (selective fact-checking with abstention — validates our unverifiable quarantine; we adopt its coverage vs selective-accuracy reporting).
  • Claim decomposition — Attributable by Construction resolves where a claim is stated; Cantheria decides whether it is true.
  • Closest neighbors — blasrodri/truth deterministically fact-checks a coding agent's claims about its own work against git; NovaFabric (arXiv 2609.12582) produces tamper-evident replayable run capsules. Truth checks self-reports in the dev loop; NovaFabric seals the record. Cantheria adjudicates claims about populations in adversarial shared records, with hunt mode, the verifier journal, and keyless probe replay — the layer neither covers.
  • Trajectory oversight — AgentDojo detectors / AlignmentCheck (bypassable per agent-as-a-proxy), TraceElephant / LongRCA / AgentLocate failure-attribution benchmarks, synapse / hansard / premier audit infrastructure. Cantheria is post-hoc claim verification, not in-flight monitoring.
  • Forensics on the same incidents — kmad/agent-swarm-forensics (collusion.wiki reproduction, one corpus, bespoke scripts). Cantheria is corpus-agnostic and additionally audits the investigators' reports themselves.

8. Limitations

  • Batch forensics, not real-time monitoring.
  • No calibrated false-positive rate — the guarantee is replayability and auditability, not e-value bounds.
  • confirmed means the committed probe passed the implemented checks — not proof of every clause of a narrative.
  • Probe quality depends on the drafting model; the mechanical fallback and repair loop mitigate but do not eliminate this.
  • Corpus coverage: aivillage + collusion.wiki exercised end-to-end; SwarmTraces loader exists.
